# Build Crew 0.4: smooth on a phone

The owner played the deployed game on his phone: "need smoother experience on a mobile phone". The reviewer's notes:
the HUD and the control deck took about 60% of a 390x844 screen, hints and chips said WASD / SPACE / I J K L on a touch
screen, the hint banner covered the pad in some rounds, stamps landed under the deck, and nobody had measured a phone.
Two things have to be smooth: the frame rate and how the sticks feel.

## What is and is not measured

There is no phone in this environment. Every number below comes from desktop Chromium with **SwiftShader** (software
GL) at 390x844, device scale 3, touch emulation and `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` 4 and 6. The throttle slows the
renderer's main thread (JavaScript, style, layout) but not the GPU process, and SwiftShader's rasteriser takes the
place of a phone GPU, so **wall-clock frame rates here are dominated by software rasterising and say little about a
real phone**. What does carry over: JavaScript cost per frame (`update`, `render` ms), long tasks, layout and style
recalculation, allocation, draw calls and triangles, and the controller's decisions. Treat the fps rows as "does the
adaptive path make the same hardware faster", not as a promise about any phone. Real-device checks are listed at the
end.

Harness: `scratchpad/bc-mobile/harness.mjs` (kept outside the repo; opens round 1 and the sandbox, spawns 20 cubes in
the sandbox, lets the controller settle 4 s, then drives both sticks with CDP touch events for 20 s and taps Grab every
5 s). The repo's own checks are `tools/frame-check.mjs` (desktop and phone profile), `tools/browser-smoke.mjs` (phone
touch and layout stages) and `tools/mobile-tests.mjs` (stick curve, interpolation, camera spring, tier table).

Reading it honestly:

* Frame rate: higher everywhere, mostly because the phone tier starts the picture cheaper and the benchmark and the
  adaptive steps act sooner. In SwiftShader the work is per pixel, so fewer pixels and no shadow pass show up as fps. The
  size of the gain on a real GPU is unknown.
* The retained-heap column is noise at this resolution (a heap sampled once after a forced GC, in a run of 15 to 200
  frames); it shows no leak either way. Garbage per frame fell in round 1 and is flat in the sandbox: what is left is
  `Yard.poses()` in `physics.mjs`, which builds fresh pose objects every physics step (not part of this change).
* Style recalcs per pointermove halved and layouts stay near zero: a stick move is one `transform` write, the stick's box
  is measured once when the finger lands, and nothing reads layout while the finger moves. Layout and recalc totals in
  the last row go up in some columns only because far more frames (and moves) happened in the same 20 s.
* Draw calls and triangles are unchanged (about 70 calls, 10 to 14k triangles; the cap is 150 and 120k).
* The quality controller's own decisions are in `window.__buildCrew.performance.quality` and the Parent Zone report.
  Under SwiftShader it walks 1.5x, 1x, 0.75x, 0.6x and drops to contact discs within about 25 s, as designed.

`tools/frame-check.mjs` (phone profile, sandbox with 64 toys and three machines, 4x throttle): p95 update 23.4 ms (limit
33.3), p95 frame 167 ms (software ceiling 350), 112 draw calls, 23k triangles, tier `phone`, DPR 0.6 after the
controller settled. The desktop profile still passes unchanged (p95 update 7.5 ms, 139 calls, 23.7k triangles).

## What changed

Rendering budget (`scene/quality.js`, `environment.js`, `effects.js`, `scene/scene.js`, `app/loop.js`, `scene/interp.js`)

* A **device tier** is chosen once from `maxTouchPoints`, the pointer type and the screen's short side (phone up to 500
  CSS px, tablet up to 1100, else desktop; `?tier=phone|tablet|desktop` forces one). Phone: DPR cap 1.5, 512 shadow map
  that is redrawn every other frame in a tighter (21 m) box with the cheaper filter, 48 particles and 18 tire marks
  (the celebration scales to the smaller pool), at most 2 physics steps a frame, interpolated drawing, no kept drawing
  buffer, `powerPreference: 'high-performance'`, no stencil. Fog is kept. Desktop is the old behaviour.
* A **benchmark** of the first 48 drawn frames (after 12 warm-up frames, at most 1.4 s) on phone and tablet: slow
  (draw over 14 ms or frames over 40 ms) starts at 1x, middling at 1.25x, and a phone with frames over 55 ms starts
  without the shadow map. The floor of the benchmark is 1x. The existing stepping (1x, 0.75x, 0.6x, half shadow map)
  continues, and a phone that is still slow at the lowest resolution switches to contact discs only.
* The loop runs fixed 1/60 s steps (3 desktop, 2 phone) and draws between the last two steps (`Interpolator`: toys by
  position and normalised quaternion, machines field by field with shortest-way angles, teleports snap), so motion stays
  smooth at 30 fps or on a 120 Hz screen. It stops asking for frames while the tab is hidden and restarts on show.
  Physics that cannot keep up drops the extra time (counted in `loopStats.behind`) rather than spiralling.
* Less garbage: the input object, pose blends, particle colors, tire marks, contact discs and toy colors are reused or
  cached; hot `forEach` closures became loops.
* Every decision (benchmark result, each resolution step, shadow changes, with the time and reason) is in the **Parent
  Zone report**, "Picture quality on this device" (tier, resolution started and now, shadows, physics budget,
  first-second test, changes this session).

Input feel (`controls.js`, `hud.js`, `style.css`, `scene/camera.js`)

* Sticks answer on the first `pointerdown`: `touch-action: none` on the sticks, joint buttons and canvas
  (`manipulation` elsewhere), no `preventDefault`, passive-compatible listeners, no sticky `:hover` lift, and the
  first-gesture audio unlock stops running on every touch after the first four gestures.
* A 12% radial dead zone and a smooth curve (`shapeStick`: gentle at first for placing a load, full power at 92% of the
  rim, direction kept so diagonals work). The knob follows the finger with `translate3d` only; the stick's box is cached
  at touch-down; a slightly larger invisible hit area surrounds each stick.
* The follow camera is a **critically damped spring** (natural frequency 12 rad/s) that also leads by the followed
  point's velocity, so a moving machine is kept centred (steady lag under 8 cm at 3.1 m/s in `mobile-tests.mjs`) and
  a stop settles with no swing. Reduced motion still snaps; hopping to another machine does not fling the camera.
* Pinch zoom is scaled to the screen and works with both sticks held; the sticks do not drop during a pinch.
* Haptics: `navigator.vibrate` for a grab (14 ms), a release (8 ms) and a placed block (short pattern), only on a touch
  screen, off in calm motion or with `settings.haptics === false`.

Layout (`style.css`, `index.html`, `hud.js`, `tray.js`; desktop layout untouched, all of it under `html.touch`)

* `html.touch` is set before first paint for a coarse primary pointer (`?touch=1` / `?touch=0` override).
* One-line top bar (back, job name, count, read aloud, pause); a slim hint strip under it (two lines at most; after 7 s it
  shows the job's goal); site tools down the left edge, machine cards down the right edge (hidden for a lone machine).
* The deck is no longer a band: two translucent sticks in the lower corners, a cluster between them (Grab, Hop, Recover,
  Horn), and the extra joint buttons as a vertical strip above the right stick (beside it in landscape). The playfield
  keeps 75% of the height at 390x844 and 66% at 844x390 (the test asserts at least 60%).
* The camera frames the machine in the free band between the top bar and the cluster (`setViewOffset`), so the machine,
  the pad and every ghost stay clear of the bar, the strip and the controls in all ten jobs in both orientations.
* The tray is a bottom sheet above the sticks (portrait) or a centre sheet between the left tools and the joint strip
  (landscape); rows scroll sideways. Sticks, joints and cards stay reachable.
* No keyboard chips on touch: stick labels say "drag the stick" (hidden after first use), hints say "drag the left
  stick" or "tap Grab", the `kbd` chips are hidden. Safe-area insets, `100dvh`, every target at least 44 px. No blur
  behind sheets on touch (a blur over a canvas that redraws every frame is expensive).

## Files outside the owned list that were touched

`src/scene/scene.js` (tier-aware renderer options, view offset, interpolated draw, shadow cadence), `src/scene/toys.js`
(no per-frame color parsing or temporary objects), `src/app/parent.js` (the quality panel), `sw.js` and the regenerated `offline.js` (new module `scene/interp.js`).
At integration `VERSION` became 0.4.0, the cache `build-crew-v0.4.0`, and the touch hint wording moved from `hud.js` to
`content.mjs` (`TOUCH_TEXT`, `touchHint`) so the voice prepares exactly the words a phone shows.

## Not verifiable without a real phone

* Actual frame rate, thermal throttling, GPU cost of the shadow pass and the benchmark's thresholds on A-series and
  mid-range Android GPUs; whether 1.5x is the right cap and whether the 14 ms / 40 ms benchmark limits are well placed.
* Haptics (`navigator.vibrate` does not exist on iOS Safari) and the feel of the dead zone and curve under a thumb.
* iOS Safari behaviour of `touch-action`, the dynamic toolbar and `100dvh`, safe-area insets in standalone mode, and the
  audio unlock after the first four gestures.
* `pointer: coarse` detection on hybrid devices (an iPad with a trackpad, a touch laptop).
