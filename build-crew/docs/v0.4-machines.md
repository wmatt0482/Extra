# Build Crew 0.4: the machines

The owner played 0.3 and asked for "more realistic machinery". The reviewer agreed: the skid steer was a small wheel
loader, all three machines shared one cab, the excavator's house did not visibly swing, and the parts were plain
boxes. In 0.4 each machine is its own model with its own cab and silhouette, still low-poly and flat-shaded in the
industrial direction of SPEC §5 (slate, unit codes, dark edges). This note lists what each machine is made of, how
its parts follow the physics joints, and what changed in the physics table.

Code: `src/scene/machines.js` (models, poses, reward art), `src/scene/kit.js` (`Parts`, the merged-mesh builder),
`src/physics.mjs` (`MACHINE` table, `SKID_BUCKET`).

## How a model is put together

The physics never changed shape: every machine is a kinematic chassis plus a tool body at the grab point, which is
`reach` metres ahead along the boom angle (`yaw + swing`) at the `lift` height, turned by `tilt` where the machine has
one. The scene places the tool exactly there and then solves the arms back to the machine, so what you see holding a
block is where the physics holds it.

- **Merged parts.** Static pieces are added to a `Parts` list (boxes, cylinders, side profiles extruded across x,
  floor plans extruded up) and merged into one mesh per layer: `body` (the paint material), `detail` (one shared
  vertex-colored material for frames, steel, tires, lights, rails) and `glass`. Every moving piece is its own group
  with its own merged meshes.
- **Instanced parts.** Wheels, track shoes, drive sprockets, hydraulic barrels and rods, and ropes are instanced
  meshes per machine, updated every frame.
- **Hydraulic cylinders.** Each is a barrel from its base pin toward its rod pin, and a chrome rod from the rod pin
  back into the barrel, so they visibly extend and retract with the joints.
- **Running gear.** Each side's travel is the chassis speed less the turn rate times the side's offset, so a skid
  turn on the spot rolls the two sides in opposite directions. Wheels spin and track shoes scroll round the loop
  (top run forward, round the idler, along the ground, round the sprocket) by their own side's travel.
- **Suspension settle.** The body (or house) sits on a sprung group that pitches against acceleration on a damped
  spring: it dips when the machine stops and squats as it pulls away. Lighter machines move more (skid 1, excavator
  0.55, crane 0.35). Arms and tools are solved in the sprung frame, so the tool stays on the physics grab point.
- **Paint.** A paint reward swaps the paint material on every `body` mesh and on the cylinder barrels. Frames, glass,
  tires, tracks, the hook block and the unit plate keep their colors.
- **Decals and horns.** Star and stripe decals are planes on paint: the skid steer wears them on its roof and on the
  outer faces of its lift arms (they move with the arms), the excavator and crane on the cab roof and the house sides
  (they slew with the house). The stock and bell horns sit on each machine's roof. `rewardArt()` draws each machine's
  own side profile with the decal in the same places.

## SS-01 skid steer

A compact, short, boxy machine that turns on the spot.

| Part | Made of |
|---|---|
| Body | Dark lower frame with a sloped nose and axle housings; painted tub, rear engine housing and hood with grille, rear door with grille slats, tail and reverse lights, side louvres, the unit plate on the rear door |
| Running gear | Four small wheels close together (r 0.42 m, wheelbase 1.2 m), chunky staggered lugs, steel rims with lug nuts; instanced, spinning per side |
| Cab | Open ROPS cage over the middle: four corner posts, side screens of bars, a header and sill, a painted roof, a rear window, amber grab handles; inside, a seat, the amber lap bar and two joystick pods with grips. Work lights front and rear, amber beacon |
| Towers | Painted rear uprights carrying the rear-link pivot |
| Lift arms | Two bent box-section arms down the body sides, with pins, hoses and a front cross tube; one moving group |
| Rear links | Two links that turn about the tower pivot (vertical-lift linkage); one moving group |
| Bucket | Wide, low box bucket built from the physics outline: floor, back, sloped side plates, spill guard, cutting edge, coupler plate and pin ears |
| Cylinders | Two lift cylinders (frame to arm), two tilt cylinders (cross tube to bucket) |
| Other | Exhaust stack with muffler and cap (right rear), front step |

**Joints.** `lift` raises the bucket on a vertical path at the fixed reach of 2.7 m; `tilt` (−0.7..0.7 rad) curls
and dumps it about the grab point. Each arm pins to the bucket at B and pivots at P; P rides on a 0.65 m rear link
that turns about R (z −0.85, y 1.2), and the arm is 3.25 m long, which keeps the arms connected over the whole lift
range. Only at full height with a full dump is B out of the linkage's reach, and the arm stretches by up to 8 %.
The skid steer has no swing joint.

## EX-02 excavator

A crawler excavator whose house visibly slews on its undercarriage.

| Part | Made of |
|---|---|
| Undercarriage (fixed) | Two track frames with five bottom rollers and two carrier rollers each, front idlers with tensioner blocks, final-drive housings, car body, swing bearing ring |
| Tracks | 32 shoes per side with grousers (instanced, scrolling per side); toothed drive sprockets at the rear (instanced, turning) |
| House (slews) | Revolving frame, rounded counterweight with rear lamps and the unit plate, engine hood with top grille and side louvres, right-hand tank box with a hand rail, exhaust stack and air cleaner, mirrors, boom foot plates |
| Cab | Front left, offset from the boom: a tall cab with a big raked front window, door glass, right side and rear glass, pillars and wiper, roof with work lights and the amber beacon |
| Boom | Bent (banana) box boom with hoses, cylinder lugs and a work light |
| Stick | Tapered stick with its heel behind the boom pin, hose, linkage pivot |
| Bucket | Side plates, curved shell, cutting edge, five teeth, pin ears, on a four-bar linkage (side link and bucket link) |
| Cylinders | Two boom cylinders (house to boom underside), stick cylinder (boom top to stick heel), bucket cylinder (stick to the linkage joint) |

**Joints.** `swing` turns the house (Shift + J/L or the Swing buttons); `lift` and `reach` (J/L) place the bucket;
`tilt` (U/O, Tilt buttons) curls it. The bucket hangs at the grab point with its curl; the boom (4.6 m) and stick
(3.3 m) solve a two-link reach from the boom foot (z 0.4, y 1.7, house frame) to the bucket pin, elbow up, which
covers the whole lift 0.7..6.2 by reach 2.6..6 range (the stick stretches by under 2 % at the far corner). The
linkage joint is solved where the side link and bucket link meet, on the bucket cylinder's side of the stick.

## CR-03 crawler crane

A crawler crane with a long lattice boom, a gantry and a hook.

| Part | Made of |
|---|---|
| Undercarriage (fixed) | Longer and wider tracks than the excavator (3.5 m frames, 0.62 m shoes), five bottom rollers, carrier rollers, idlers, car body, slewing ring |
| Tracks | 33 shoes per side (instanced, scrolling); drive sprockets (instanced, turning) |
| House (slews) | Deck, machinery house with louvres and a roof walkway, four counterweight slabs with lifting lugs and the unit plate, boom foot plates, exhaust stack, hand rails on the roof edge and rear |
| Cab | A long low operator cab at the front left: raked windscreen, long side window, inner side window, roof skylight for watching the boom, work lights, amber beacon |
| Gantry | A-frame at the back of the house with its sheaves |
| Winch drum | Open drum between two frame plates, with flanges, spokes and a bright rope clamp; it turns as the hoist pays rope in and out |
| Lattice boom | 13 m, four chords with taper at the foot and head, zigzag lacing on all four faces, cross bars, foot pins, head with two sheaves and a tip light; one merged mesh |
| Ropes | Two boom-hoist cables (gantry to boom head), two load-line falls (head sheave to hook block), the hoist line from the drum up the boom |
| Hook | Hook block with cheek plates, stripes and its sheave, a hook, four chains, and the lifting magnet under it |

**Joints.** `swing` slews the house (J/L); `reach` (U/O) luffs the boom: its angle is solved so the load line leaves
the front of the head sheave right above the grab point, so the falls hang plumb; `lift` (I/K) raises the hook. The
magnet's face sits 0.55 m above the grab point, on a 1.2 m block's top. The drum's turn is the rope paid out. At the
far corner (reach 9, lift 10) the hook block still hangs 0.8 m below the head. The wrecking ball still hangs from
the grab point on its own spring.

## Physics table changes (`src/physics.mjs`)

- `SKID_BUCKET` is new and exported: the skid steer bucket's outline, shared with its model.
- The skid steer's **side plate colliders** are now convex hulls of that outline (their top edge slopes from 0.16 m
  at the back to −0.40 m at the cutting edge), instead of 0.7 m tall boxes, so they match the sloped side plates you
  see. The floor and back plate colliders are unchanged. A tool entry may now be `{ hull }` as well as `[size, offset]`.
- A `swings` flag per machine: the skid steer has no swing joint, so Shift + J/L no longer orbits its bucket round
  the chassis. The excavator and crane swing as before.
- No reach, height or tilt range changed, and the grab point rule is unchanged. The excavator's bucket and the
  crane's magnet still have no colliders (they only grab), as before.

## Budget

Measured with `tools/frame-check.mjs` (sandbox, 64 toys, all three machines, Chromium + SwiftShader, 1024x768):

| | 0.3 | 0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Draw calls | 140 | 96 |
| Triangles | 23,684 | 32,728 |
| p95 CPU update | 9.9 ms | 9.3 ms |
| p95 render | 9.4 ms | 8.0 ms |

Draw calls fell because each machine's static parts merge into a few meshes (the three machines went from 92 calls
to 48). Triangles rose with the detail and stay well inside the 120k cap and the ~40k working target. Track shoes,
rods and ropes do not cast shadows, to save shadow-pass draw calls; frames, bodies, booms, buckets, wheels and
barrels do. SwiftShader wall-frame times depend on the shared runner's load and are not a device measurement.
