# Build Crew 0.6: the harbor, the hillside and the wheel loader

Flight two of SPEC §3: jobs 11 to 20 on two new work sites, the scene moving between sites without a reload, and the
WL-06 wheel loader as a fourth free machine. The yard (jobs 1 to 10 and the sandbox) is unchanged: `physics-tests.mjs`
still hashes its data against the 0.4.0 snapshot, and its floor, scenery and tool heights take the same code paths.
Nothing new is behind Lab coins: every job is finished with the skid steer, excavator, crane and wheel loader, which
every builder earns in the jobs. The Lab's dump truck and forklift join a job as an optional extra when a builder owns
them (`optional` on the job), never as a need.

Code: `src/sites.mjs` (new: the ground every layer agrees on), `src/physics.mjs` (sites, slopes, mud, water, the
`LOADER` geometry, `TRACTION`), `src/content.mjs` (jobs 11 to 20, rewards, hints, spoken lines), `src/missions.mjs`
(the grass rule), `src/scene/environment.js` (site themes, `disposeEnvironment`), `src/scene/scene.js` (`setSite`),
`src/scene/machines.js` (the loader model, pitch and roll, new decals and horns), `src/app/*` (board, HUD, hints),
`tools/solution.mjs` (plans for jobs 11 to 20), `tools/try-round.mjs` (new: solve jobs headless while developing).

## Sites

A site is data in `sites.mjs` (bounds for a machine's centre, fence walls, the ground height function, and whether it
is a terrain site) plus a theme in `environment.js` (palette, fog, lights, sky, daylight colours, sign, ground builder
and an ordered list of scenery features). `Scene.setRound(yard)` calls `Scene.setSite(yard.site.id)`, which does
nothing when the site is already on show; otherwise it clears the round, disposes the old site and builds the new one.

**Disposal.** Each site builds through its own material cache (`Object.create(kit)` with its own `materials`), so the
site's materials can be freed without touching the machines' and toys'. `disposeEnvironment` removes the site's root
group and frees: every instanced mesh made by `kit.batch`, every geometry that is not one of the kit's shared unit
shapes (the terrain meshes, the cones, the sign plane), the lights (the sun's shadow map), the sky's shader material,
every material of the site cache, and the site's textures (gravel, concrete, the sign). The toys' speckle texture now
belongs to the scene (made once per page from the yard's gravel), so toys look the same on every site. The quality
controller is handed the new sun with the old sun's shadow decisions (map size, shadows on or off). The browser smoke
checks that the scene is rebuilt exactly when the site changes (`state.siteBuilds`).

### The harbor (jobs 11 to 15)

A concrete quay cast in 4 m bays with joints, a yellow safety line and an edge kerb, the quay wall down into seafoam
water with rubber fenders and chains, bollards, the moored barge (slate hull, rust-orange band and gunwale, plated deck,
mooring lines, draught marks), two old dock piles south of it, a rust-orange ship-to-shore crane straddling the quay
north of the fence with its boom over the water, container stacks in muted rust, teal, olive and slate, a corrugated
transit shed, lamp masts, two ships at anchor and a breakwater light. Physics: one quay slab with its top at y = 0, the
kerb, a harbor bed under the water, the barge as a fixed hull with its deck 0.25 m below the quay and a 0.35 m gunwale,
and the piles flush with the quay. Machines keep 1.4 m back from the edge (site bounds). A load that falls in the water
splashes and comes back to its start; that is a recovery, never a fail.

### The hillside (jobs 16 to 20)

A lower yard (y = 0), a 12.5 degree haul slope up to a terrace 2 m higher, a 31 degree rock shoulder along the east,
a ravine 2.3 m deep cut into the terrace, two mud patches, gravel work areas and a gravel haul road, grass elsewhere
(the strip between the two lower work areas is a marked grass verge). Physics: one Rapier **heightfield** (80 x 80
cells of 0.5 m, sampled from `hillsideHeight`, the function the machines ride on); a ray from above lands within 6 cm of
the ride height everywhere a machine or a block can stand (the ravine's rock faces are only as sharp as the half-metre
grid). The scene draws the same function at the same half-metre cells inside the fence and 2 m cells out to the hills,
with a skirt where the two meet: sage grass (drier on the slope), warm gravel, terracotta rock banded like strata where
it is steeper than 25 degrees, dark mud with glossy puddles; pines, far hills, boulders, the fence following the ground,
amber verge posts, white delineators along the haul road, a site cabin.

## Slopes, mud and the machines (physics.mjs)

Machines stay kinematic and never tip (SPEC §1's assistance), but on a terrain site they now sit on the ground:

* **Pitch and roll.** Each step the chassis is seated on the ground under it: its ride height from the centre and its
  pitch and roll from the ground under the ends and sides of its wheels or tracks (`TRACTION[kind].length`, `width`),
  eased so a kink in the ground does not snap it. The kinematic chassis turns by the same rotation and the model turns
  with it (Euler YXZ: heading, pitch, roll). In the yard both are always 0, so nothing there changes.
* **Never slides.** A machine with no input does not move on any slope it can climb (tested: ten seconds parked
  mid-slope, every machine, no drift at all).
* **Climb limits.** `TRACTION[kind].climb` is the steepest ground (degrees) a machine can drive onto: skid steer 20,
  excavator 24, crane 17, wheel loader 22, dump truck 16, forklift 9. A move onto ground steeper than that is refused
  in either direction unless it leads to gentler ground, so a machine waits at the foot of a bank, at the lip of the
  ravine, and never drives over an edge. The 12.5 degree haul slope is climbable by every machine but the forklift; the
  31 degree shoulder by none (tested for all six).
* **Climbing costs speed.** Up a slope a machine keeps `1 - 0.55 * grade / limit` of its speed (never under 30 %).
* **Mud.** In a mud patch wheels keep 45 % of their speed and tracks 72 % (`MUD_FACTOR`; tested on the skid steer,
  loader and excavator).
* **Tools on a slope.** On a terrain site the lift is measured from the ground under the machine. The skid steer's,
  excavator's and crane's grab point stays level, `reach` metres out (the operator keeps the load level), and the model's
  arms solve back to it in the pitched frame. The loader, dump truck and forklift, whose tools are rigid on the chassis,
  carry their tool with the chassis's pitch and roll (`toWorld`, `toolQ`, `chassisQ`).

## WL-06 wheel loader

A free machine (MACHINE rows in `content.mjs`, `physics.mjs`, `scene/machines.js` and `app/hud.js`, engine pitch
55 Hz in `sound.js`), earned at job 16 for the sandbox and used in jobs 16 to 20.

| | |
|---|---|
| Controls | Left stick drives; I/K (right stick up and down) lifts; J/L (right stick sideways, Tilt buttons) tilts the bucket. Hint: "The wheel loader bends in the middle to steer. I and K lift the bucket, J and L tilt it." (touch: "... Drag the right stick up to lift, sideways to tilt.") |
| Articulated steering | `artic` eases toward the steering (up to 0.55 rad) and back when the stick is let go. The front frame (arms and bucket) turns about the hinge by half of it, so the bucket swings out to the side of a turn; the grab point follows. It turns slowly on the spot (40 % of its turn rate), as a loader does by bending and creeping. |
| Lift on an arc | The arms pivot 2.1 m up on the front frame and are 3.2 m long, so the bucket rises on a real arc: furthest out (3.8 m) at the pivot's height, 3.5 m at the bottom and 2.8 m at the top. `loaderReach(lift)` is that arc. |
| Bucket | 2.9 m wide, a floor, a back and two sloped side plates (`LOADER.bucket`, colliders and model from the same outline). It scoops the way a real one does: lowered to the ground short of a block, tipped forward so the cutting edge is down, crept in, then curled back to hold it (the scripted solution and a physics test do exactly this). |
| Model | Rear frame: rails, engine hood with side louvres and a top grille, counterweight with grille, lights and the WL-06 plate, fenders, fuel and hydraulic tanks, steps and hand rails, stack, air cleaner, beacon; the cab just behind the hinge on four ROPS posts with glass all round, roof with work lights, mirrors on arms, seat, column, wheel and joystick pod. Front frame: rails, axle, tower plates, pivot shafts out to the arms with braces, fenders, work lights. Two lift arms outboard of the bucket with pins, hoses and a cross tube; two lift cylinders, a tilt cylinder and two steering cylinders across the hinge; four big lugged tyres (the front pair turn with the front frame). Star, stripe and the new decals on the roof, the hood sides and the arms. |

## Voice

Every new line is a fixed string in `content.mjs`: the ten intros ("Job sixteen. Haul road. Haul four loads of fill up
the haul road to the blue pad at the top."), goals and reward lines (`REWARD_SAY`: "the Chevron decal", "the wheel
loader", ...), the loader's hint in both wordings, the hints for a slope too steep, mud, the grass and a Lab extra, and
the toasts for a splash, the grass and the three new decals. They are in `SPOKEN` / `VOICE_LINES` (122 lines, 7.3 k
characters), prepared at builder select like the rest; `voice-check.mjs` checks them statically and plays a harbor
and a hillside job (`sites` scenario) asserting they are heard in the natural voice and never the device voice.
