A motocross-style race with flips, big air and rivals: 30 worlds, 8 vehicles, 5 cups, and a Hill Climb after every finish where each right answer is a burst up the mountain. Math Gates stop the race: your child hears a times-table fact and types the answer, and a quick right answer is a big boost toward the podium. Game three

Fraction Fright

Zombies rise out of the fog toward the fence, each hiding right at its fraction's spot, and the road is a number line. The scout calls a fraction ("three quarters!"); your child drags along the road and lobs a slime grenade at that spot. On the spot, the zombie is blasted into cartoon goo (Gentle mode in the Parent Zone turns that into a cure and a neighbour walking home). Off the spot, the true spot lights up with the fraction drawn as jumps from 0, so every throw teaches where the number is. Twins, a boss and combos keep it lively. A short Zombie Lab comes first.

Grade 4 fractions on the number line No timers, accuracy only
Why it works

The science we build on

  1. 1

    Retrieval practice

    Pulling a word out of memory builds it far better than copying it. In Spell Jump: the word is spoken, never shown, and your child spells it from memory. In Trail Blazer: the answer to each Math Gate is typed on a number pad, never picked from choices.

  2. 2

    Spaced practice

    Practice spread over days lasts longer than cramming. In Spell Jump: words come back on a schedule (next day, 2 days, 4 days) until they stick, and old lists return weeks later. In Trail Blazer: facts return over the following days the same way, mixed with the other tables instead of drilled one table at a time.

  3. 3

    Immediate self-correction

    Fixing a mistake right away, by yourself, beats correcting a whole list later. In Spell Jump: after a miss comes Cover-Copy-Compare: see what differs, copy it, then spell it once more from memory. In Trail Blazer: a missed fact shows its answer and a strategy hint (for 7 × 9: "10 × 7 is 70, take away 7"), read aloud, then hides it, and your child types it from memory to open the gate.

  4. 4

    No misspelling exposure

    Seeing wrong spellings makes kids spell worse, even a week later. In Spell Jump: there are never wrong spellings to pick from, and a wrong attempt is cleared within seconds. In Trail Blazer: a wrong answer is cleared within half a second and the correct fact takes its place.

  5. 5

    Intrinsic integration

    Kids learn more, and play longer, when the learning is the game rather than a toll before it. In Spell Jump: spelling right is what powers the jump. No "do homework, then play". In Trail Blazer: facts open the gates and fill the boost tank, so knowing them fast is how you win the race.

  6. 6

    Fluency

    Knowing a fact is not enough: it should come back in about 3 seconds, so working memory is free for the bigger problem. Practice that rewards speed, after accuracy, builds that automaticity (Hasselbring 1988; Woodward 2006), and new facts stick best when rehearsed in between known ones (incremental rehearsal, Burns 2005). In Trail Blazer: a right answer within 3 seconds is a ⚡ Quick boost. There is never a clock on screen, and a fact counts as fluent only after quick answers on two different days. A short Pit Crew Check before the first race finds where your child is, and the game keeps checking as they race, so practice stays on the next tables that are ready.

Five minutes, once a week

Get started as a parent

  1. Open Spell Jump, tap Parents, set a PIN, and create a family code. Join with that code on your kids' devices so their progress follows them: one code syncs all three games.
  2. Paste this week's spelling words in Parents → Words of the week, one per line. To add a sentence, put it after two bars, like neighbor | | My neighbor has a dog.
  3. Kids play about 8 words a day. Check the Report on Friday to see how far each word has come, and its progress chart to see the weeks add up (every game's Report has one).
  4. For math, open Trail Blazer → Parents → Facts this week and pick the times tables (×2 to ×12, with ÷ if you like), or type your own problems one per line, like 7 x 8 or 56 / 7. Each race asks 4 facts; 12 a day is the daily mission.
  5. For fractions, open Fraction Fright: your child plays the short Zombie Lab once, then picks a street (the ★ is the suggested one). Parents → Fractions shows where they are on the ladder, and the Report shows the week's accuracy.

All three games share the same players, family code and parent PIN: set them up once in any game.

What's stored online: first names, word lists, spelling progress, Trail Blazer progress (math facts practiced, vehicles, medals and stars), Fraction Fright progress (fraction throws, streets, the yard) and a hashed parent PIN, under your family code. Nothing else. Word lists, sentences, the math facts and the fraction names being practiced are sent to Google's text-to-speech service to make the voice clips; nothing about the kids is. A daily anonymous count of devices, runs and races (no names, no ids) tells us the site is being used.
