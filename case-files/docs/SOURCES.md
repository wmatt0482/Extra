# Case Files: sources for real-history claims

Every real-history claim in the Erie Canal arc is listed here with a URL, and the same claims sit in each case's `facts`
array (checked by `tools/content-tests.mjs`). Characters, dialogue, boat names (except the Seneca Chief), weights, and
the plots are fictional.

**How these were checked.** The content session could search the web but could not open most pages directly (the network
allow-list blocked wikipedia.org, eriecanal.org, canalmuseum.org and similar hosts), so each claim was confirmed from search
excerpts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. The parent review step should spot-check the rows marked
**spot-check** (secondary or thin sources). Where a detail could not be confirmed it was left out; see "Dropped or avoided".

## Case 1: The Case of the Missing Waybill (Rochester, June 1826)

| Claim in the case | Source |
|---|---|
| The canal crossed the Genesee River at Rochester on a stone aqueduct finished in 1823, about 800 feet long (sources say 802 or 804 feet, so the text only says "an aqueduct" and "stone") | https://eriecanal.org/Rochester-1.html , https://crookedlakereview.com/articles/67_100/75june1994/75shilling.html |
| The first aqueduct was later replaced by a second aqueduct, now the Broad Street Bridge | https://structurae.net/structures/broad-street-bridge-1842 |
| Mules or horses towed boats from a towpath; a young driver, often a boy, was called a hoggee | https://scdemocratonline.com/stories/hoggees-on-the-towpath,57342 |
| Boats were cleared at a collector's office, and toll records list boat, master, articles shipped, where bound and weight, so a paper listing the cargo belongs in the toll process. The word "waybill" for that paper is the story's working term; the records call it a clearance. **spot-check** | https://snaccooperative.org/vocab_administrator/resources/8210835 |
| A toll collector worked at Rochester (cash books of a Rochester toll collector survive from 1826) | https://snaccooperative.org/vocab_administrator/resources/8316569 |
| A line boat carries cargo and a packet boat carries passengers. **spot-check** | https://stagecoachdays.blogspot.com/2012/12/the-packet-and-stagecoach.html |

## Case 4: The Case of the Heavy Flour (Rochester, the 1840s)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Tolls were based on cargo weight. The boat was weighed on a weigh lock scale and its registered empty weight was subtracted to find the cargo weight | https://eriecanalmuseum.org/weighlock-building-home/ , https://www.eriecanal.org/UnionCollege/The_Weigh_Lock.html |
| The toll per ton differed by kind of cargo | https://bookwormhistory.com/2015/11/24/how-did-they-levy-tolls-on-the-erie-canal/ |
| Rochester was one of five weigh-lock locations (Albany, West Troy, Utica, Syracuse, Rochester) | https://genealogy.bio/reference/New_York/new_york054.html |
| Weighmasters worked under the toll collector; collectors recorded clearances | https://snaccooperative.org/vocab_administrator/resources/8210835 |
| Tolls ended in 1883 (used only in the arc's background; not in the text) | https://bookwormhistory.com/2015/11/24/how-did-they-levy-tolls-on-the-erie-canal/ |

The story's numbers (55, 25 and 20 tons; 300 barrels; ten barrels to the ton) are fictional but chosen to be roughly in the
real range (a flour barrel weighed about 200 pounds; canal boats carried tens of tons).

## Dropped or avoided (could not verify, so left out)

- Exact number of arches in the Rochester aqueduct (sources say 9 planned and 11 built; the text does not give a count).
- When the Rochester weigh lock began operating. It existed (1860 gazetteer, historical marker), but the start date was not
  found, so case 4 is set only in "the 1840s".
- The number of Seneca Chief kegs (sources say one keg or two barrels), so the text says "a keg".
- Cannon salutes relayed along the canal for the 1825 opening, and the "81 minutes" figure.
- How many miles a hoggee walked a day, hoggees' hours, and team-swapping routines on packets.
- Roving bridges and towpath changing sides; "bank watchman" as a job title in the 1820s; how many feet each Lockport lock lifts.
- Anything about Haudenosaunee or Seneca land (no verified, respectful source gathered), so the arc does not mention it.
- The real Rochester toll collector, weighmaster and lock tenders by name; all named characters are fictional.
