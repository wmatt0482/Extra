# Spell Jump: learning design and the evidence behind it

Every mechanic below exists because a technique has evidence for it. Where the evidence is thin or comes
from a different age group, that is noted. This file is the rationale; SPEC.md is the build contract.

| Technique | Evidence (short) | Where it lives in the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval practice** (spell from memory, from audio, never from a visible word) | Rated highest-utility learning technique in Dunlosky et al. 2013; a 2018 classroom study found retrieval practice beat rainbow writing for spelling, including 5 weeks later | Every jump starts with a word spoken aloud and spelled from memory. No word is ever on screen while the child spells it. |
| **Spaced (distributed) practice** | Rated highest-utility in Dunlosky et al. 2013; spaced retrieval helps children's word learning | Leitner boxes with expanding intervals (same session → next day → 2 days → 4 days). A word is "Mastered" only after 3 first-try successes on at least 2 different days. Past lists come back 2 and 4 weeks later. |
| **Test-Study-Test** (pretest, study only what you missed, retest) | About twice as many studies favor test-study-test over study-test (Horn-era and later); Gentry's recommended weekly cycle | New list → a quick **Qualifier** (pretest). Known words skip ahead; missed words become Focus Words and get most of the practice. **Friday Test** at the end. Parent report shows pretest vs test. |
| **Immediate self-correction** (correct right after each word, child does the fixing) | Horn's corrected-test research; later single-case studies found correcting after each word beat correcting after the list | A miss is checked immediately. The child sees which letter positions differ, then fixes it themselves. |
| **Cover-Copy-Compare** | Established, evidence-based self-management procedure (Joseph et al. 2012 review) | After a miss: Compare (differences marked) → Copy (type it from the model) → Cover (model hidden, type it once more from memory). |
| **Avoid exposure to misspellings** | Reading misspellings reduces later spelling accuracy for good and poor spellers, lasting at least a week (Dixon 1997; Brown 1988) | The child's wrong attempt is shown for at most 3 s with differences marked, then cleared. The game never offers wrong spellings as choices ("pick the right one" is deliberately absent). |
| **Simultaneous Oral Spelling** (say letter names while writing) | Gillingham-Stillman multisensory routine with reported benefits for young and struggling spellers | Each typed letter is spoken by name. After a miss the correct word is spelled aloud letter by letter with the tiles lighting in sync. Letter rings in the air say their letter. |
| **Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check** for new words | RCT (Year 3) found it as effective as a phonological program on taught words, weaker on transfer | First encounter only: Look (word shown with the pattern chunk highlighted and syllables) → Say → Cover → Write → Check. |
| **Pattern / morphology focus** | Explicit pattern instruction transfers better to new words than rote routines | Optional "pattern of the week" (e.g. `ough`, `-tion`) and per-word syllables from the parent; highlighted during Look and Fix steps. |
| **Interleaving** | Third graders made fewer errors after interleaved vs blocked practice (Klimovich & Richter 2025) | The scheduler mixes patterns and mixes in 20% past-list words; never the same word twice in a row. |
| **Immediate feedback, limited new items per session** | Feedback beats none; practitioner guidance to introduce only a few new words per session | Instant check on LAUNCH; at most 5 new words per day; a daily mission of about 8 retrievals (8 to 10 minutes of play). |
| **Intrinsic integration** (the learning action is the game action) | Children learned more and chose to play 7× longer with intrinsically integrated versions (Habgood & Ainsworth 2011) | Spelling sets the launch power and nitro; letters are the rings; the Friday score sets the Mega Run power. No "do homework, then play". |

Sources consulted (secondary summaries unless noted): Dunlosky et al. 2013 (APS); The Learning Scientists 2018
spelling study; Horn 1947 corrected test (via 1978 Iowa guide and later single-case studies); Joseph et al.
2012 CCC review; Dixon 1997 thesis and Brown 1988 (JEP) on misspelling exposure; Gillingham & Stillman SOS
(Bradford/Swindon help sheets); Waikato RCT on LSCWC vs phonological strategy; Klimovich & Richter 2025
interleaving; Habgood & Ainsworth 2011 intrinsic integration; Gentry on test-study-test.
